Pick the body that fits the entry, and write one article rather than a tour of everything the sources hold. The opening and the close are fixed by the rules; what changes is the middle. Use the shape, never its headings: name each section after this article's own content. If the Extra Rules name a shape, use that one.

## One spine

A shape is a sequence of jobs, not a template to fill. These hold for every shape:

- **One block the reader can run.** A shape that teaches a tool carries at least one fenced block quoted from a source and whole enough to run: the command and its output, the manifest, the file. Elisions and undefined names are fragments: introduce the name in the prose above the block, never by editing the block, because the ledger matches its words against the page.
- **One boundary.** Before the close, one line or one paragraph on when this is not the choice to make, and what to reach for instead. A recommendation with no boundary is an advertisement.

- **One outcome.** Before the first sentence, state in one line what the reader will have done or understood by the end. Every section serves that line; a section serving a different outcome is a different article, so cut it.
- **One path.** When the shape walks the reader through work, the steps are the only path. An alternative install, a second tool or a "you could also" is one sentence in the prerequisites at most, never a step inside the walk-through and never a numbered sub-step.
- **Names carry.** Every name a later step uses — cluster, namespace, Secret, ensemble, persona, run, URL — is one an earlier step created, spelled the same. A second page's example that names its own objects is reference, not a step: adapt it to the names this article created, or cut it.
- **One job per section.** Two sections that explain the same thing, two examples of the same point, or two closes are one section. The section the title promises gets the most room; every other section carries one concrete example and stops.
- **Numbers are one sequence.** If the step headings are numbered, every step is numbered 1..n with no gap and no duplicate, no sub-step joins the sequence, and the close is not a numbered step. If the headings are not numbered, number none.
- **A step ends on what the reader sees.** After a command, say what success looks like when the source material carries it, otherwise the one thing to check. Then the sentence that says why that step is there. Never a note about how the article was written.

## Introduction to a thing

For a project, tool or release the reader has never seen.

- Why this category exists at all — the problem, in the reader's world
- What else is out there, and where it stops
- Why this one, in one sentence
- What it does best — the features that matter, each with the concrete reason
- How it is built — the architecture, at the level a newcomer can follow

## A decision and what it cost

- The constraint that forced a choice
- The options that were on the table
- The one that was picked, and the reason
- What it cost
- What it bought, with numbers

## An incident and the rule it produced

- What broke, and how it showed up
- How it was found
- The cause
- The fix
- The rule that came out of it

## A comparison

- What each thing actually is
- One table on the dimension that decides the choice, its figures quoted from the sources
- Where each one wins, with the numbers
- Where each one fails
- Who should pick which

## A myth corrected

- The belief
- Why people hold it
- What is actually true
- The evidence
- What to do instead

## Getting started

For a tool the reader will run, where the point is the first working result.

- What the reader needs, complete before the first command: cluster, CLI, credentials, permissions, a model or key. Nothing new appears after step 1.
- The smallest thing that works, as the numbered steps and nothing else: one action per step, the command as its own block, then what success looks like when a source carries it, then one sentence on why. A field or flag the reader must change is named in that sentence.
- The trap that breaks this exact walk-through, as a paragraph in the step where it bites. It is not a section of its own, and it does not introduce a second example.
- Where to go next: the first change to make, or the page that continues the work. Never a recap.

## The state of a thing

- What changed
- The three or four things that matter, each with the reason
- What it means for someone doing this work
- What to watch next
