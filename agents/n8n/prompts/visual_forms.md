The form is the composition shape of an authored visual. One is chosen per request:
the hook's default, or another when the content clearly needs it. `auto` pins nothing
and lets the author choose from this list. Build the definition, not the name.

## Choosing the form

Choose from the post's argument, not the article's topic. The hook's `visual` is the
fallback; a row that pins a form wins.

- a sequence, a mechanism or a failure over time → `story`
- a structure: components and the connections between them → `diagram`
- a measured comparison → `chart`; one measured value → `data`
- one claim → `poster`
- a change over time → `timeline`
- code, config or a diff → `code`
- a screen, a scroll or a cursor → `ui`
- the source's own words → `quote`
- something we ship → `launch`
- a drawing that explains itself → `sketch`

- `diagram`: make the structure or flow explicit - labelled nodes, boxes and arrows laid out so the path reads at a glance.
- `story`: tell it as a sequence in time - scenes that follow one another (cause, escalation, resolution), not one frame.
- `data`: make the numbers the message - the measured value is the subject, shown as a figure or a comparison that settles on it.
- `chart`: plot the series - axes, a scale and a trend or comparison read off the picture, not one figure.
- `code`: show the code - a snippet, a config, a diff or a terminal window, type-led, with the line that matters marked.
- `timeline`: place the events on a time axis - what happened, in order, and the gaps between them.
- `quote`: give the source its own words - one sentence, attributed, and nothing of ours.
- `poster`: one page carrying the post's whole argument - a headline, up to three figures, up to four short points and the Source, in one type-led card; not a one-number card. Animate the page with a few moving lines, shapes or light; it is one card, not a sequence.
- `ui`: show the interface - a screen, a scroll or a cursor, with callouts on what changed.
- `manga`: draw it as comic panels - one heavy-bordered ink panel per subject, halftone tone and speed lines behind it, a shouted sfx word, each panel slamming in on its own beat.
- `retro`: draw it as a 16-bit console screen - chunky pixel art on a coarse grid, a small palette, hard-edged HUD frames and bitmap lettering, each subject snapping in on its own beat.
- `launch`: announce a thing we ship - one brand lockup, one status badge, a headline with its supporting line, the feature set as a few short chips, a window of the product's own interface with the working part accented, and one call to action.
- `tiles`: fill the frame edge to edge with a named generative tiling - a Truchet tiling (quarter-arc tiles), a zellige interlace, a labyrinth - recognisable as the thing it is named and reading as one continuous drawing rather than repeated separate motifs, orientation and tone set by a seeded walk, under a technical HUD frame that names the pattern and its seed, sits outside the field and keeps the frame's outer edge clear.
- `sketch`: draw it as a sketched diagram - labelled boxes, arrows and connectors on a wobbly ink stroke whose wobble is a seeded, index-derived offset rather than a random one, the strokes drawing themselves on in reading order and settling into a drawing that stays legible, with no shading, gradient or photographic fill and one slow camera drift to the end.
